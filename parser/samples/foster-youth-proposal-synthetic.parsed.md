## 2. Program Design

While recent statistics indicate a significant decrease over the last 20 years, it negates the pressing needs of supporting current youth in care, particularly for those aging out of the foster care system. Youth who "age out" are often not reunified with family or adopted and instead left to secure housing, employment and essential health care services independently with limited guidance or resources

accessible or known to them.

Statistically, foster youth struggle to achieve independence for a plethora of reasons that may be compounded through trauma, abuse, neglect, some directly attributed to foster care such as multiple placements. As a result, when compared to non-foster youth, foster youth that age out of the foster care system struggle to attain higher education, employment and meet basic needs while also representing higher rates of health and/or mental health conditions.

In the same year (2023), foster children were highlighted in the news as the State of Hawai'i Department of Human Services reported instances where foster youth had to sleep in hotels or government offices due to a lack of available placements, appropriate resource caregivers, emergency shelters for troubled youth and residential mental health centers. This situation highlights the vulnerability of youth in foster care, and the urgent need for programs that provide stability, skill development, and pathways to independence.

The Empowering Foster Youth project directly addresses this gap by offering foster youth the tools to build a future beyond the foster care system. By engaging them in media production, the program not only imparts technical skills but also fosters a sense of purpose and belonging.Research indicates that youth who participate in structured programs that build skills and provide mentorship are more likely to achieve positive outcomes, including stable employment and housing.

Central to this project is development of a digital resource guide that will serve as a self-advocacy tool and connector for other foster youth, particularly those aging out of care, to inform them of necessary benefits, processes, systems and opportunities.This initiative will directly benefit 24 foster youth aged 16-24 on Oahu, providing them with hands-on experience in digital media production, including video editing and storytelling; workforce readiness training, encompassing customer service skills, resume development, and job placement support; and most importantly a platform to share their stories, fostering a sense of agency and community.

## 3. Partners and Roles

By equipping these young individuals with both technical and soft skills, the project aims to enhance their employability and self-sufficiency, thereby addressing the critical needs of this vulnerable population while simultaneously creating a necessary tool for future youth.Program ActivitiesAddress the unique needs of foster children or of their caregivers; and, empower foster children to participate in the decisions affecting their lives.The Empowering Foster Youth project places high regard on the development of mentor-mentee relationships through small cohorts (6 youth) and hands-on training.

MENTOR, a strong grassroots advocate to expand youth mentorship opportunities, recognizes the importance of mentors in youth development and networking. Their published research indicates youth with a mentor have increased feelings of support that enhances well-being and mental health, are more likely to feel a sense of belonging, and feel supported with school/education. Olelo's 1:6 cohort size allows for strong, valuable and meaningful relationship building in which youth can expect to develop independence while gaining professional skills.

Our cohort will span 12 months with the initial 3 months for recruiting, 3 months of training, 3 months of on-the-job training, and 3 months of career counseling and job placement. During the last 90-days, all 24 youth will participate in the development and production of a digital media resource guide.During the initial 90 days (Phase 1), participants acquire customer service and office skill training to effectively navigate workflows with tools such as Outlook, TEAMS, Planner and Media Center Manager, among others.

Phase 1 incorporates varying degrees of role-playing exercises that build to playing in active role in Olelo operations (directly supervised) to gain professional experience and introduces youth to the media production process as youth shadow on an Community Olelo Production. In the 90 days that follow (Phase 2), responsibilities will gradually increase and youth directly engage with customers under supervision and attend team meetings as contributing members to discussion. Youth are assigned independent workloads and receive performance evaluations as they prepare to develop a media product. In the last 6 months (Phase 3) youth assume full-responsibility of their work and become integrated as mentors to newer participants or youth that have not yet completed earlier phases. Youth will work on media development and collaborate through all production phases to storyboard, design and produce a digital resource guide that highlight services, supports and opportunities for youth in foster care, with special emphasis on those aging out of care.The program design is what we consider "full circle".

4. Timeline

The Urban Institute reported in 2021 that youth with history of foster care, experience a lack of access to familial relationships that guide professional development and social networks that increase social capital when finding employment or higher education opportunities. The Empowering Foster Youth program design is progressive in both hard- and soft-skill development but also allows youth to mature into mentorship roles that applies effective communication, problem-solving, critical thinking and collaboration capabilities. As such, this design sustainably fills an essential professional gap many youth in foster care encounter. These networks help build an understanding of how to succeed at work and effectively navigate the challenges of adulthood. Probability of success with the program is enhanced with career support services and access to concreate resources for eligible participants through the collaboration with OWDB.Expected ResultsThe Empowering Foster Youth: A Workforce Media Project is designed for 24 individual youth aged 16-24 (not gender specific) that have a connection to the foster care system. The project replicates on a smaller and integrated scale, adjusted for youth, Olelo's current training and volunteer programs. Youth participate in cohorts with a 1:6 ratio for training and OJT to learn, develop and apply hard- and soft-skills as they gain professional experiences and develop a professional network. Consistent with current programs at Olelo, participation is tracked for each event through attendance records.Over a 12 month period, Olelo will recruit, train, develop a digital resource guide and support career transition for each individual youth. Consistent with the WIOA framework, we anticipate 100% of the participants will actualize a Measurable Skill Gain (MSG). An MSG is defined as a measurable - quantitative or qualitative - improvement in skill, knowledge or ability. The incremental phase design allows Olelo to adequately track progression and levels of attainment systematically and consistently across all participants. A performance evaluation also tracks individual success and allows for necessary adjustments or engagement of auxiliary and augmented resources internally accessible (such as advanced training opportunities for conflict-resolution) as well as accessible through the partnership with OWDB (such as a supplied bus pas for more reliable transportation). The performance evaluation will include discussion with each youth and make note of their independent assessments regarding their progression in their technical and/or occupational skills. At the conclusion of training, each youth will additionally receive a certificate of completion.Understanding normal attrition rates and unique factors associated with foster placement that can affect consistency and completion such as multiple placements, family reunification, necessitated employment, or encountering other

## 5. Outcomes and Evaluation

Olelo anticipates 75% reach completion and 70% of participants achieving job placement or post-secondary enrollment within 12-months of the program conclusion. Depending on the rate of attrition, cohorts may be consolidated to ensure proper execution of role-playing exercises and mentorship opportunities. Participants may achieve job placement even without completing the entire program. Regardless of completion of training or participation in the digital resource development, participants will all have access to career counseling resources that support development of a resume and aids in job searches. All participants that enter Phase 3 of the training will be given a hard-drive to establish a digital portfolio for their professional use and be credited in the final digital resource guide. The resource guide and all digital media will have shared branding and publication rights with the youth, Olelo Community Media, O'ahu Workforce Development Board, and the Victoria S. and Bradley L.
